Opening note / 01

One song.
One session.

Looking at the market like an artist: observing, forming expectations, and staying open to what happens next.

How do you approach something you can’t fully predict? You look at it like an artist.

A familiar pattern gives me something to consider. A change in rhythm asks me to look again. I can form an expectation and still leave room for the day to surprise me. Each session gives me a chance to compare what I imagined with what actually happened.

I think about the market the way I think about music: something that unfolds through time.

There is an opening. A rhythm begins to take shape. Something repeats, then breaks. Energy builds, disappears, and returns in a different form. By the end of the session, the day has become something I could not fully hear at the beginning.

That is where my theory starts.

What if every market day were a song?

Each song would have the same duration. The time would stay fixed, while everything happening inside it could change. A different pace. A different mood. A different relationship between tension and release.

Some sessions feel patient. Others feel restless. A move can gather momentum, stall, reverse, or return to where it began. I hear those changes as musical possibilities: a phrase that keeps repeating, a rhythm that suddenly opens up, a passage that never quite resolves.

I want to explore that connection one market day at a time.

MaloSound gives this practice a place to live. During the trading week, Monday through Friday, the journal follows two moments in the day. Before the open, I write about the context: what is happening, what has my attention, and what I am listening for. After the close, I return to the session through a song and a reflection on the price action that inspired it.

The first entry holds my expectations. The second gives me a way to respond to what actually happened. Reading them together may reveal where my attention was useful, where I missed something, or where the day surprised me.

I am still developing this theory. I want to make the thinking public while it takes shape, including the questions and revisions.

The constant duration matters to me because it creates a shared frame. Each day gets the same space, yet no two days have to fill it in the same way. Over time, the songs and writings can become a record of both the market and my changing interpretation of it.

This is how I want to use MaloSound: to pay attention, put the experience into words, and give each session a sound.
