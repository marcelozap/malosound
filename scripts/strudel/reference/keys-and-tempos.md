# Keys and tempos

## Note names in Strudel
Sharps: `fs cs gs ds as`   Flats: `ef bf af df gf`
Octave number follows: `a1` is low bass, `a4` is around middle.

## Your lanes

| Lane | BPM | Feel | Key suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drake half-time | 152 | chest, dark, spacious | A minor or F# minor |
| Latin house | 122 | hips, driving | A minor |
| Reggaeton / dembow | 90–100 | brighter, swung | A minor |
| Blues shuffle | 88 | triplets, loose | E or A (open strings) |

## The tempo problem, resolved
152 half-time and 90–100 reggaeton are **incompatible grids**. Three ways out:

1. **Stay at 152**, use trap-era Bad Bunny instead of reggaeton. Cleanest fit —
   you keep the Spanish delivery and the menace without fighting the tempo
2. **Drop to ~96** and make it a reggaeton record. Valid, but it's a different
   song. Headlines energy is gone
3. **Beat switch** — verses at 152 half-time, last third at ~96 dembow. Most
   "who is this" moment available to you, and native to the reference

## Key character (rough, but useful)
- **C major** — bright, open. Fights menace. Don't put dark Spanish verses here
- **A minor** — dark without being cartoonish. Default for both main lanes
- **F# minor** — heavier, more claustrophobic
- **E** — best key on guitar. Open strings ring. Blues home

## Guitar: transposing a Strudel bassline
Move every note by the same interval. If the pattern is in A and you want E,
everything drops a fourth (or rises a fifth) — including the chord stabs.
Move them together or the harmony breaks.
